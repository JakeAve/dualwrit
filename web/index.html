A way of writing English with one letter for each sound, made in Utah in the 1850s. There are no silent letters and no spelling rules to memorize: if you can say a word, you can write it.

Write something

Type in either box and the other follows. Choose a spelling style on the right.

Deseret
Spelling style

Underlined words can be said two ways. Press one to switch it. Your choice is written into the English as a tag, like read{past}; a dotted underline is a guess.

Use your keyboard to type Deseret letters, hold (Alt/Option) for alternate letters

The letters

Each letter makes the sound marked in its example word, every time. A capital is the same shape drawn larger. Under each word is how it is spelled in the style chosen above. Press a speaker to hear the word.

Gliding vowels

Two vowel sounds run together. The 1869 printers dropped the last two letters and spelled those sounds with two letters each.

Consonants in pairs

Each pair uses the same mouth shape: first as a whisper, then with your voice on.

    Practice

    Spell one word at a time. The boxes are the sounds whose letters you have met: type the Deseret letter for each. The rest of the word stays in Latin until its letters arrive, a new one each time you have the hang of the last.

    Pronunciations come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, which is American English. The 1869 style follows the spelling of the Deseret Book of Mormon printed that year.
