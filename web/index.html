A way of writing English with one letter for each sound, made in Utah in the 1850s. There are no silent letters and no spelling rules to memorize: if you can say a word, you can write it.

Spelling style

Underlined words can be said two ways. Press one to switch it. Your choice is written into the English as a tag, like read{past}; a dotted underline is a guess.

The letters

Each letter makes the sound marked in its example word, every time. A capital is the same shape drawn larger. Under each word is how it is spelled in the style chosen above.

Gliding vowels

Two vowel sounds run together. The 1869 printers dropped the last two letters and spelled those sounds with two letters each.

Consonants in pairs

Each pair uses the same mouth shape: first as a whisper, then with your voice on.
